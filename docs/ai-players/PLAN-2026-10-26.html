1 · What exists today, read from the code

Everything below was read from game/engine/pilots/, game/room/, cloud/game-room.mjs, cloud/ai.mjs and the AI-2 section of docs/plan-to-done-2026-09-30.md. Where Rob's ask and the code disagree, the difference is named.

Lobby settings
Three: Easy, Normal, Claude (level: "llm"). Hard does not exist in the engine yet. The 1 to 5 policy in game/contracts/pilot-policy.mjs (Learner to Expert) is the simulator's and the Forge-era API pilot's contract, not the table's.
Easy and Normal
Both are L1, the scored pilot (scored-pilot.mjs), with L0 (house-pilot.mjs) as the floor for every choice L1 does not score. Normal takes the best-scoring plan. Easy draws by softmax at temperature 4 among plans within 12 points of the best, so it is usually right, sometimes second-best, never absurd by design. The seed decides its draw, so replays agree.
What L1 scores
Which spells to cast (a knapsack over the mana), where a spell is aimed (the worthiest permanent on the right side), which creatures attack (damage, trades with exact combat math, the swing back it exposes), which block, and whether to keep the opening hand (hypergeometric odds of the third land).
Targeting today
L1 scores one attack plan per opponent and picks the best plan; the opponent is chosen by the plan's score, so the choice of target is a by-product of damage and trades, not a threat reading. L0 attacks the lowest life. Neither looks across the table at ramp, growth or engines. The simulator's pilot-policy.js already names four readings of "the leader" (weakest, biggest board, closest to winning, most dangerous engine) and shows they disagree; that is the seed of the threat model below.
The Claude seat
L1 Normal for every decision, and at a close call (two best plans within 1 point, in its own main phase or at declare-attackers) the room offers the decision to Claude Haiku 5.5 with L1's pick as the hint, thinking off, 25¢ a game, the house pilot answering whenever the model cannot. Every answer is on the tape, so replays never ask the model.
Saltiness
A deck dial, 1 to 5 (1 Extremely friendly, 2 Friendly, 3 Assertive, 4 Disruptive, 5 Any legal winning mechanic) in crankmagic-game.js. There is no AI-player saltiness, no persona, and no "-1" scale in this repository; the -1 convention is in the project files outside it. This plan adopts a signed dial for AI players (section 3) and maps it onto the deck dial where decks are built.
"AI-8"
No AI-8 appears in the repository (the AI series runs AI-1 to AI-6). It is read here as the LLM seat: the Haiku 5.5 seat through the AI door. If AI-8 names something else, say so and the Veteran section is re-based on it.
Information
Every pilot is handed the seat's projection, the engine's offers and the decision, and public card facts. The projection carries player names but never a seat's kind (person or AI) or its setting; the LLM brief replaces names with "Seat n". No pilot has ever been able to tell a person from an AI, and this plan adds the test that proves it stays so.
Game records
A finished game's record lives with its table in the Durable Object: seats, result, history, refusals; on a playtest table also the seed, pod, tape and journal. A seat reads its own; there is no list across tables and no admin view.
Access
Cloudflare Access (invite-only, Google sign-in) gates everything; ai_allowlist or AI_EVERYONE=on gates the AI door; spend caps per person and in total over 24 hours, and per game for the LLM seat. There are no user groups.
Cards playable
6,405 engine card definitions under game/engine/cards/ (6,255 at the last production release); the deck generator (deck-generator.js) builds from the catalog by role quotas, bracket, budget and the Saltiness, Speed and Competitiveness dials.

3 · The definitions

Each card lists the value of every variable. today marks what the code does now; change marks what this plan adds. The saltiness dial is signed: -2 group hug, -1 friendly, 0 assertive, +1 disruptive, +2 cutthroat, +3 and +4 salty (stax and lock pieces, the same seat hit every turn). Easy and Normal are capped at -1; Hard at 0; only a persona can raise the cap (section 7).

Easy

The turn at hand. Usually right, sometimes second-best, never stupid.

  • A Horizon today this turn and the swing back next turn, nothing further.
  • B Threat change the four foundation signals only: total life, biggest hitters, cards reaching the board, ramp rate. Equal weights, rounded to a rank. No engine or commander reading.
  • C Target change the top of that rank, held for the turn; ties go to the lowest life (L0's rule).
  • D Attackers today L1's plans; change the floor under absurd: a plan whose every attacker dies to a free block and deals nothing is removed before the draw, at every difficulty.
  • E Blockers today L1's exposure and crackback terms; no counted reserve.
  • F Own engine none. It plays what fits the mana.
  • G Mana today the knapsack, largest first.
  • H Hand today keep at 60% odds of the third land.
  • I Quality today softmax at temperature 4, band 3.
  • J Saltiness cap -1. Removal goes at the biggest thing, never at a land; no lock pieces held for a seat.
  • K Person bias none. L Compute rules only, no model.

Normal

The game at large, weighted lightly. Friendly, but it breaks a chain it can see.

  • A Horizon change this turn first; the game at large (its own engine, opponents' engines) at a low weight.
  • B Threat change the four foundation signals, plus an engine score: cards on the board that compound (mana doublers, draw engines, token makers with payoffs, untappers, recursion). It sees a chain once two links are down, not at the first.
  • C Target change balanced between the foundation rank and the engine score; removal prefers the live link of a chain over the biggest body.
  • D Attackers today L1's plans with the absurd floor.
  • E Blockers today L1's exposure terms; change a light reserve: one blocker held when any opponent could present lethal next turn.
  • F Own engine change knows its deck's chains (section 5, L2 lite) and scores a cast higher when it completes a link; weighted low.
  • G Mana today the knapsack. H Hand today as Easy.
  • I Quality today the best plan, temperature 0.
  • J Saltiness cap -1, as Easy.
  • K Person bias none. L Compute rules only, no model.

Hard

Comprehensive and balanced. Reads commanders early, assigns a target per attacker, counts the blockers it needs.

  • A Horizon change the game at large: its own chains, every opponent's engine, and the next full turn cycle in combat.
  • B Threat change every signal: life, hitters, growth, ramp, engines (seen at the first seed card), and the commander's own threat class from the catalog (commander strategies, EDHREC rank, bracket).
  • C Target change a weighted threat score per opponent, re-read every decision; removal aimed at the permanent whose loss cuts the most from that score.
  • D Attackers change a preferred defender computed per creature (where it does the most effect, least likely to die), then the set solved against the reserve.
  • E Blockers change a counted reserve: the number and size of blockers held from a weighted probability of what comes at it before its next turn (section 4).
  • F Own engine change plays toward the highest-compounding, fastest-resolving chain its hand and board allow; weighted high.
  • G Mana change the knapsack, with mana held for an answer it holds when a threat score crosses a line.
  • H Hand change keep odds plus a chain check: a hand with a link and its lands keeps at lower odds.
  • I Quality the best plan, temperature 0.
  • J Saltiness cap 0 (assertive). Never stax by default; a persona may raise it.
  • K Person bias none. L Compute rules only, no model.

Veteran (the agent)

Hard's computation, reasoned over by the cheapest model. Replaces today's "Claude" seat.

  • A to J inherit Hard, and the chosen persona's overrides.
  • L Compute change Hard runs first and produces the threat table, the per-attacker targets, the reserve and its top-k plans. Claude Haiku 5.5 is asked to choose among those plans, with Hard's pick as the hint, and to say why in one sentence. Its answer is validated (pickOf) and taped; Hard answers whenever the model cannot.
  • What it is never told: who is a person, any opponent's difficulty or persona, or how the deterministic difficulties are built. The brief carries no seat kinds and no settings; a test reads the brief for them.
  • Budget the existing 25¢ a game cap, the consult points widened from close calls to every declare-attackers, every removal aim, and the opening hand.

Rob's wording, interpreted. "Neither easy nor normal go beyond a -1 on saltiness" is read as a cap at -1 on the signed dial (friendly or kinder), never past it toward salty. If the project files define the dial the other way round, only the sign flips; the structure holds.

4 · The threat model, shared by every difficulty

One module, game/engine/pilots/threat.mjs, pure over the seat's projection and public card facts, returns a threat table: one row per living opponent, the signals, and a weighted score. The difficulty supplies the weight vector; the module never knows which difficulty is asking. This is the deterministic answer to "who is the biggest threat, and where do I have the biggest effect on their ability to play".

The signals

SignalDefinition, from the projectionEasyNormalHard
LifeShare of starting life gone: (40 - life) / 40, poison counted at 10 each; commander damage taken from me counted.1.00.80.6
HittersPower on the board, evasion weighted (flying, trample, menace, double strike as L1's keyword worth), as a share of my life: how fast they can kill me.1.01.01.0
GrowthNonland permanents that entered their board over the last turn cycle, from the public history, against the table's median.1.00.90.8
RampMana sources on the board over the turn number, against the table's median; rocks and dorks count as sources (manaColors).1.01.01.0
EngineCompounding permanents, from the card facts' roles and the graph corpus: doublers, draw engines, token makers with a payoff, untappers, recursion, extra combats. Normal needs two links on board; Hard scores one seed.00.61.2
CommanderThe commander's threat class from data/commander-strategies and its EDHREC rank and bracket: a known engine or combo commander scores from turn one, on the board or in the command zone.00.21.0

Easy ranks by the sum and keeps the rank for the turn. Normal and Hard take the weighted sum each decision. A second number, effect, scores an action against a seat: for an attack, the damage through as a share of their life; for removal, the drop in that seat's threat score if the target leaves. The target is where threat × effect is highest, so a huge threat behind a wall of blockers loses to a smaller one that can actually be hit. This is the "biggest damage or biggest effect on their ability to play" rule in one formula.

The floor under a stupid attack

Before any draw, at every difficulty, an attack plan is removed when every attacker in it dies to a block the defender can make for free and nothing gets through. Easy's softmax then draws only from plans a person would consider. The bench's blunder checklist gains this case so a regression shows as a count.

Person bias: the invariant

The threat table's inputs are the projection, public card facts and public history, none of which carries a seat's kind. A test (tests/engine-threat.mjs) builds the same board with a seat marked person and marked AI at the room and asserts byte-identical threat tables and identical pilot answers. The same test reads the Veteran brief and fails if any seat kind, level or persona name appears in it.

5 · Hard's combat: targets per attacker and the blockers it keeps

A preferred target per creature

For each untapped creature that may attack, and each opponent it may attack, Hard computes effect × threat for that single attacker against that defender's untapped blockers (exact combat from fight and defend), minus the worth lost if it dies. The best defender is its preferred target. Attackers are then solved as a set against the reserve: the greedy improvement L1 already uses, now over attacker-defender pairs instead of one defender for the whole plan, and still within meetRequirements and the attack budget.

The reserve

Before attacking, Hard counts what may come at it before its next turn:

for each opponent o still to act before my next turn:
  attackers(o) = untapped creatures of o with power > 0, less those that must block me
  P(o swings)  = sigmoid(k · (their effect on me - their effect on the table's top threat))
  need(o)      = blockers that stop lethal, from defend({attackers(o), my untapped, my life})
reserve = smallest set of my creatures such that
  Σ_o P(o swings) · [need(o) unmet by the reserve] ≤ risk(difficulty)
sized so that the reserve holds a blocker that survives each trampler, and a flyer or reach for each flyer, when any

risk is the difficulty's appetite: Hard 0.25, Normal 0.6 (one blocker when lethal is possible), Easy none (L1's exposure terms only). The reserve is removed from the attacking set before the per-attacker solve, so Hard never sends the creature it needs home. Vigilance creatures count in both.

Its own chains (L2 lite, Rob's "the deck's formulas")

At launch the room hands every AI seat its deck list. chains.mjs reads the deck's chains from the graph corpus (data/graph.json, the synergies the deck intelligence lane already scores) and the card facts: each chain is a set of cards, a completion condition and a payoff rate (cards, mana or damage per turn once complete). A cast that completes a link scores + payoff × weight(difficulty) / turns-to-complete. Normal's weight is low and it only credits chains with a link already on board; Hard credits the fastest-resolving, highest-compounding chain its hand and mana allow from turn one. Easy never reads it.

6 · The Veteran agent

The Veteran is not a new pilot. It is Hard, run first, with the model reasoning over Hard's output. This keeps the engine's determinism (the answer is taped, never recomputed), keeps the model's job narrow (choose among plans Hard already judged legal and sensible), and keeps cost under the existing per-game cap.

StepWhat happensFile
1 · Decision offeredThe room reaches a decision for the Veteran seat.game/room/room.mjs
2 · Hard computesThreat table, effect per action, per-attacker targets, reserve, chain credits; the top-k plans (k = 4) with scores and one-line reasons in words.scored-pilot.mjs at setting: "hard"
3 · Consult or answerConsult at declare-attackers, removal aim, the opening hand, and any close call; everything else is Hard's at once.agent-pilot.mjs (consult)
4 · BriefThe seat's view as today (no names, no kinds, no settings) plus Hard's plans, threat table and reasons; the persona's disposition line.game/room/agent-brief.mjs
5 · ModelClaude Haiku 5.5, thinking off, effort low, JSON {indices, why}; the instructions say to prefer Hard's pick unless a listed reason outweighs it, and to play the persona.cloud/ai.mjs (PILOT_SYSTEM)
6 · Validate and tapepickOf keeps only an offered plan; Hard answers otherwise. Logged in ai_calls with the plan chosen and whether it matched Hard.cloud/game-room.mjs

What it never learns. The brief has no seat kinds, no opponent settings, no persona names of others, and no description of how Easy, Normal or Hard are computed. The system prompt describes Magic and the persona, not CrankMagic's difficulty mechanics. The invariant test in section 4 reads every brief for these.

Why the cheapest model is enough. The model is choosing among four plans that already carry the numbers; its value is in weighing the reasons (a persona's grudge, a commander it recognizes, a line Hard scored close) rather than in computing. The eval in phase 4 measures the Veteran against Hard over seeded games; if it does not beat Hard at the cap, the consult points narrow until it does.

7 · Personas

A persona is chosen after the difficulty and sits over it. It has a name, one sex (male or female), an avatar, a short description that carries its disposition, and overrides on the variables. The schema is data (data/ai-players/personas.json), validated by a suite, so new personas are rows, not code.

The lobby's seat picker becomes difficulty, then persona, with the avatar and blurb shown; the table shows the persona's name and avatar at the seat.

8 · The default-deck matrix

Each difficulty and persona pair gets one default deck, built by the deck generator from the engine-playable catalog (the 6,405 definitions today, growing with each card load) so every default deck plays at the table without a refusal. The matrix is data (data/ai-players/decks.json), rebuilt by a tool whenever the catalog or a persona changes, and each cell records the criteria it was built from and the commander chosen.

10 · The game-log admin panel

Today a record lives with its table. The panel needs an index across tables and an export built for a session to mine. Three parts:

  • Index. At game end the game room writes one row to a new games table in D1: match id, table id, started and ended, turns, seats (kind, level, persona id, deck name and commander), result, the AI spend, and the record's size. The owner's route GET /api/admin/games lists it with filters (date, difficulty, persona, result) and paging.
  • Detail. GET /api/admin/games/:match returns the full record as a playtest table's does today (seed, pod, tape, journal, history, refusals), plus the AI seats' decision records: for each decision, the threat table, the plans scored, the pick, and for the Veteran the model's answer and whether it matched Hard.
  • Download all. GET /api/admin/games/export?since= streams one CrankGameLog@1 JSON object per line (NDJSON) with a header line that states the schema, the build and the field dictionary, so another session can read the first line and know how to extract wins by difficulty and persona, blunders by type, threat-choice accuracy (did the seat it hit lose next), consult agreement rates, and spend per game. Each line is self-contained; the file is bounded by date, and a manifest line closes it with counts.

The admin panel itself is a page under Settings visible only to the owner (the Workshop lane builds the screen; the engine lane builds the routes and the writer). The brainstorm doc Rob named ("CrankMagic Admin Panel — Brainstorm") was not found in Drive under that title; the panel is scoped here to logs, AI calls, groups and feedback, and widens when the doc is at hand.
