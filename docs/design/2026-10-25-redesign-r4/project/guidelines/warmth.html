CrankMagic r4 · guidelines

The warm table

Rob's reference makes you want to sit down and play. These ten principles are why, each with what the template does about it. The template keeps the reference's room, type, drawers and voice, and keeps CrankMagic's own palette: the five mana colors each have one job, and no single mana color owns the frame.

The reference

Ten principles

1

A place, not a page

In the reference. A walnut table under a lamp, books, dice, a brass compass. You could pull up a chair.

Play happens on a table you could sit at: wood, felt and warm light from above, falling into shadow at the edges. The interface floats over the place. The place is never a flat color.

In the template. .cm-study on --table-wood, --table-grain, --table-light, --table-shade; seats on .cm-mat. Drawn from noise, so there is no image to load.

2

Warm light, neutral chrome

In the reference. The panels are near-black and calm; the warmth is all in the room around them.

Warmth comes from materials and light (walnut, brass, parchment, the lamp), never from tinting the chrome. No mana color owns the frame: players take their commander's colors as their own, so the frame stays neutral and color arrives with the cards. This is the one thing we do not take from the reference: its green felt and green buttons.

In the template. Grounds and panels are the skin's neutrals. The felt is --table-felt, a warm charcoal. A deck's surfaces take its commander's --mana-* tints; nothing else does.

3

The name stays ours; brass is the table's metal

In the reference. The app name sits in gold capitals beside an emblem, and the table has brass fittings: the rings around each portrait, the compass.

The app is CrankMagic, and its name keeps its own mark: the CrankMagic wordmark beside the gear logo, exactly as it is today. The reference's gold lives on as brass on the table, the rings around portraits, and never marks a value, so it is never mistaken for a price.

In the template. .cm-wordmark (the app's Oxanium mark in --color-aether, unchanged); --table-brass for fittings on the table. Money keeps --color-money.

4

Two voices of type

In the reference. Engraved roman capitals at the top of the screen; a warm, open sans for everything you read and do.

Roman capitals name places and things: page titles and the deck title. A humanist sans does the work: reading, choosing, counting. Section titles are spaced capitals in the sans, each with a small icon.

In the template. Cinzel 600 through --font-display and --font-hero; Lato 400 and 700 through --font-body; .cm-section-title.

5

People at the table

In the reference. Every seat is a face in a ring, a name and a big life total.

Seats are people, not slots. Each shows a portrait, a name and the number that matters at a glance. Here the portrait is the commander's art, because that is who the player is at this table.

In the template. .cm-portrait in a --table-brass ring; .cm-seatmini on a mat, .cm-seat in the HUD views, .cm-boardrow in a drawer.

6

The cards carry the color

In the reference. The hand is big real card art; the spell, the target and every history line show their art.

Card art is the most magical thing on the screen, so show it generously and let it bring the color. A thumbnail beats a word wherever a card is named.

In the template. .cm-pick (art, name, meta), .cm-history (art, line), the hand at full card size.

7

Say what happens before it happens

In the reference. "Choose a creature to exile", a Target then Review step, and the consequence written out before you confirm.

A decision names the task as a plain sentence, shows the step you are on, shows what you picked, says what will happen if it resolves, and offers exactly two buttons: the step forward and the way back.

In the template. h2 task, .cm-steps, .cm-pick, .cm-consequence, .cm-decide-actions (one primary, one secondary).

8

The table can always be reclaimed

In the reference. Both side panels have a tab with a chevron on their inner edge.

Side drawers hold the decision and the stack. Each folds to a labeled tab at the screen edge, so the table can fill the screen and nothing is lost. A decision is never hidden: with its drawer folded it shows in the center slot.

In the template. .cm-drawer with .cm-drawer__tab, left, right and bottom; see Play surfaces.

9

Soft depth, few lines

In the reference. Panels float over the table on soft shadows; mats have an inset edge; dividers are faint and few.

Depth comes from soft shadow and a 1px light line, never from heavy borders. Corners are 12 to 16px. Padding is generous. A divider separates groups, not rows of the same thing.

In the template. --radius-card, --radius-tile, --color-line, the drawer and mat shadows.

10

Quiet status in the margins

In the reference. Seats filled, settings and the clock sit small in the top corner.

Ambient facts live at the edges, small and out of the way: who is here, the time, the settings. They never compete with the decision.

In the template. A top-right row of compact on-art buttons and small text; the center top holds the view switch, a .cm-seg whose selection is ink.

Using the template for a new page

For the walkthrough screens and wireframes, in order:

  1. Start every page from a frame in frames/ and keep its rail, head and footer.
  2. Name the page in Cinzel (h1); give every section a Lato h2, or a .cm-section-title when it sits in a drawer or panel head.
  3. Put one sapphire primary on the screen, the next step of the journey. Everything else is secondary or quiet.
  4. Wherever a card is named, show its art (.cm-pick, a tile, a row thumbnail).
  5. Write every decision as a sentence, show the step, show what will happen, and give two buttons.
  6. For anything at the table, set it on .cm-study with seats on .cm-mat; put side detail in a .cm-drawer that folds to its tab.
  7. Keep the chrome neutral. The only mana color on a surface is the commander's own, through its tints and its cards.
  8. Check every frame in all three skins and both registers, at 1440 and 390, against the contrast pages.
