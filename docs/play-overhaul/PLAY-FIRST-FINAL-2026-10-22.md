The historical master is design evidence, not a command to implement every extension or to resume paused engine work. The earlier bounded visual-quality trial is removed: integrate the existing prototype engine, then verify the integrated product. No new approval checkpoint for that adoption.

## Intended experience

Play is a Commander card game with a sense of place. Cards are the primary objects; a person can understand the current question, see the relevant cards and costs, make their own decision, and follow what happened. Focus offers a spacious, green, game-oriented composition. Full-screen offers the same truthful game in a polished, optionally surreal scene. Table keeps the useful whole-game overview. Presentation never acquires rules authority.

The complete journey is website Play → pre-game lounge → a table's people/deck/readiness lobby → launch and opening-hand choices → live game → results and records → another game or deck refinement. Opening Play must show the lounge, including a prominent Resume when a seat exists; it must neither create a table nor silently leave or claim a seat. Direct invitations continue to the intended table. This resolves the master's tension between “Play opens lounge” and automatic live-game return: one visible Resume action preserves both orientation and continuity (CM-B01–02).

The full-card rule concerns card presentation, not forcing every card in a 100-card board on screen simultaneously. Scrolling and deliberate stack expansion preserve access while readable defaults preserve comprehension. Overlapping attachments expose titles, with the complete card available immediately through inspection. No layout crops the art/text inside an individual card.

## Visual direction after the October 23 screenshot review

The earlier Decision Desk and Table Companion concepts remain visual references. The first supplies Focus's quiet green play field, clear action hierarchy and balanced columns; the second supplies Full-screen's tangible materials and calm, opaque tools over a scene. Their invented card text and simplified board states are not functional specifications. The real game must retain the master's complete decision inventory, all three views, readable full cards, zone access, history, attachments, counters and optional analytics.

Apply the visual language through shared typography, spacing, panel surfaces and borders as the planned views are completed. Use the Crank Magic title family for headings and a readable body family for rules and controls. Favor deep green/charcoal panels with restrained warm trim and a single clear primary action. Keep seat colors and state cues meaningful, preserve the chosen accent theme, and reserve strong glows for focus, selection and actionable objects. Full-screen can keep imaginative scenery while opaque control panels keep text and choices legible over it.

Keep Actions and Boards in the same left-side structure, with Stack and History consistently on the right and optional tools recoverable through Settings. Allow modules to collapse or float without hiding a pending choice. Place the source card, concrete question, selected target/cost and confirmation together; contextual controls on a card and the Actions pane must submit the same authoritative choice. Card inspection must not accidentally commit a game action.

These are design decisions informed by the mockups and Microsoft's guidance on [consistent UI navigation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/112) and [visible focus](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/113). A reskin is accepted against actual crowded-board, decision, collapse/expand, keyboard and scene screenshots, with the existing functionality crosswalk beside the review. It must improve hierarchy without trading away a legal choice or putting information behind decoration.

The immediate screenshot corrections are intentionally small: opponent previews occupy the available Focus gap; central Full-screen actions are about 12% smaller (49px desktop buttons, with a 44px minimum on narrower screens); the camera offers tilt, zoom and card sizing, and empty-ground dragging changes tilt only. The broader visual pass remains step 4 below, alongside the functional view work rather than a separate rewrite.

## Sequence to first-final

1. **Adopt now:** port the proven Babylon table construction behind `CrankStage`, retain live DOM cards/actions and all saved table designs, provide tilt and a usable graphics failure path. Replace automatic New table/return routing with the lounge and verified Resume. Keep invitation routes unchanged. Prove the released asset graph, not just the prototype server.
2. **Unify actions and inspection:** adopt left Actions/Boards across all three views; improve labels/source/targets and waiting states; share card/name inspector; remove duplicated prompts. Preserve every legal fallback while designing the specialized decision widgets. Integrate useful preserved priority-card work selectively against current main.
3. **Card handling and density:** readable hand and filters/spacing, independent zone scroll, exact-state stacks, 30-degree tapping, relationship layout and control provenance. Request missing projection fields before grouping anything ambiguous. Benchmark realistic crowded games separately from very large token counts.
4. **Modules and complete visual language:** floating/docked panels and recovery, optional analysis, title font coverage, Focus composition, oak/obsidian art direction, coherent sound and bounded callouts. Camera and scroll gestures never steal card selection. Keep Table available.
5. **Close the whole journey:** real chat/presence service, reliable invitations/deck preparation, launch, reconnect, durable results/logs including shared decks, and rematch. Reuse existing capabilities; repair demonstrated gaps rather than rebuilding them. Discovery and leaderboard remain later, so finding a table initially means a direct invitation or Resume.
6. **Commander conformance and acceptance:** engine owners separate standard/variant policies, close the meaningful-choice inventory and required payment/provenance contracts. Run complete human, mixed and solo games, then review results and repeat. First-final is not declared while required chat, records, fidelity or choice gaps remain.

Steps 2–6 define the encompassing completion scope. This adoption does not claim they are all complete. Payment draft support (ROB-74), control provenance (ROB-172), and the paused decision gaps (ROB-190) stay explicit dependencies. No deployment is implied by this local integration.

New checks compare actual Babylon-projected table corners to DOM projection; tilt/resize/change designs and ignore older saved rotation; measure bounded material/texture counts; enter/leave repeatedly; force graphics load/context failure while keeping the choice operable.
* Screenshots: 1280×720 and larger desktop, 390×844 and phone landscape, oak/obsidian, normal/crowded boards, pending choice and collapsed/open panes. Inspect screenshots for clipping, alignment and overlays; assertions alone are insufficient.
* Prove new assertions detect deliberate mutations, restore code, and pass again. Run feature-wiring, asset versions and release dependency checks plus the required pre-push scan. Run full CI once on the finished PR, not repeated local full gates.
* Report failures and what was not exercised. Desktop browser emulation cannot establish physical touch, a phone GPU's frame rate, a live multi-person session, or subjective audio quality. Targets of 100ms feedback/60fps desktop/30fps phone require named hardware measurements.

## Why these practices fit the intent

Stable control locations and redundant visual/text cues reduce reorientation between views and after interruption; they do not remove optional depth. Microsoft's Xbox accessibility guidance supports consistent navigation, clear UI context and input alternatives ([XAG 112](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/112), [XAG 113](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/113)). The integrated test remains the evidence that a particular layout works here. Babylon's camera/projection model supports a shared scene/DOM camera; its presence alone proves neither visual quality nor performance. Commander rules and meaningful choices remain the room's responsibility.
