An intelligent Commander deck creator & card library

A collection is not a list. It is a set of claims.

One copy of Sol Ring; four decks that want it. A spreadsheet holds the number — it cannot hold the argument. CrankMagic knows which deck has a claim on each copy you own, which card is sitting in a sleeve standing in for one you have not bought yet, and what the rest would cost to finish.

Everything runs in your browser. No account, no server holding your collection, and the whole app works offline once it has loaded.

What it is for

Four questions a deck box cannot answer.

Do I own this?

Every card carries its counts — owned, reserved, ordered, watched — and the decks that have a claim on it, or the words Not assigned when none do. The same line appears on the card page, in the graph's pop-up and on the table.

Why is that card in my deck?

Because it is standing in. A card in the box that the list does not call for is a Substitute: it holds the seat until the real one lands. CrankMagic counts them and says which seat each is keeping warm.

What do I still need to buy?

Derived, not typed: the deck's targets, minus what you own, minus what is already in the post. Priced, sorted, printable, and it shrinks the moment a parcel arrives.

Would something I own do instead?

A card on your bench that fills the same slot is offered beside the one you were about to buy, with the saving attached. Owning beats buying whenever the swap is straight.

Step one

Two ways in.

Start from nothing and let the app build you a hundred, or start from the cards you already own. Both end in the same place: a deck with a list, and a library that knows where every copy is.

Build → the Lab

Draft a deck from a commander

You have an idea and no list. The Lab drafts one, then measures it.

  1. Pick a commander — any that can legally lead a deck, not a shortlist. Inspect one before you commit to it.
  2. Set the starting point, a budget cap and a bracket. The cap warns while you draft and offers a way through at the end rather than silently refusing to finish.
  3. Run a draft. It previews; nothing is saved until you press Save this deck.
  4. Measure it. The simulator plays it out and reports what it saw — and says plainly when it cannot see how the deck wins, which is more useful than a number it invented.
Cards → Import

Bring in what you already own

You have shoeboxes and a spreadsheet. Import turns them into a library.

  1. Import a list or library — a CSV, an .xlsx, or a block of names pasted from anywhere.
  2. Names resolve against the real card catalogue, so a flavour name finds the card it actually is. Anything ambiguous comes to a fix-names screen where you can paste a link instead.
  3. Everything that is not claimed by a deck becomes your Bench.
  4. Already have a list for a deck? Make the deck, paste its hundred, and Finalize — that is the moment the app starts reserving your copies against it.

Step two

Cards

U

Four tabs, three lenses Cards

The tabs are Library, To buy and Orders — the states from the diagram above, each with its own page. The switch beside them changes how you look at whichever tab you are on; the data does not change, only the instrument.

  • List — sortable, filterable rows with a status on each and an Add/Buy menu. The one for “find the thing”.
  • Sheet — the spreadsheet you came from, editable in place, for fixing a price or a count without opening a dialog.
  • Table — the physical one. Your cards as piles on a slate mat, grouped by status or by anything else. Click a pile and it fans out into a drawer along the back row; drag a card out of the drawer onto any pile to move it, and the board stays put while you do.

Nothing is written until you confirm Staging

Step three

A deck, from defining to complete.

Every deck sits at one of four stages. The tile's bar is its hundred, split by where each copy actually is.

DefiningThe list is still being written. No reservations yet — a draft claims nothing.
BuildingFinalized. Cards are still to buy or still to walk in from the bench.
PlayableSubstitutes make up the hundred. You can sit down with it tonight.
CompleteEvery reserved copy is the real card, in the box.
W

Five tabs on a deck page Decks

The header is the summary — commander, stage, bracket, mechanics, latest measured score. The row beside it is the work: add what you own, buy what you do not, log the game, measure.

  • Overview — Progress counts the physical deck, ready to add, ordered and to buy; Cost is what finishing would cost against your cap; the Next line says what to do first.
  • Cards — the hundred by type, with where each copy stands.
  • Guide — commander, strategy and a SWOT. Marked as structural: it is text matching, not modelled availability, and the page says so rather than letting you assume otherwise.
  • Upgrades — the working list and the Upgrade Path, each row naming the slot it replaces, its price and why. Promote takes it; Decline retires it so it stops being offered.
  • History — the game record and every measured run.

Step four

Discover

The part that is not a spreadsheet at all.

G

The connected card catalogue Discover

Every card is a node; every join is a claim you can open and read. Not “these get played together” — this one untaps that one, this one causes the trigger that one fires on. Follow it outward and the deck you did not know you were building appears.

  • Three modes. Navigate re-centres on a card. Inspect opens a pop-up with the card, its counts, the decks that claim it and an Add/Buy menu — tap the same card again to put it down. Select ticks cards for a group.
  • Primary Purpose is the term with the gold ring: the one job a card is in a deck for, decided by a fixed ladder from finisher and extra turn down through removal, draw and ramp to its body and its tribe.
  • Loops only walks just the joins that continue or pay off a loop; Loops this card is in lists every cycle of four cards or fewer through the focus, each step named and the missing pieces dashed.
  • Trace lights a deck from its commander outward: only the joins that serve its strategies, loop-backs in gold, the cards it never touches ghosted on the outer band.

Structural links (shared mechanics and roles) and observed co-play (EDHREC) are different kinds of evidence, and the app keeps them apart. Neither claims a simulated improvement.

Step five

Play

R

The play space, and the lobby Play

A board with three zones and shelves down both sides, where moving cards between decks, bench and groups is dragging them rather than filling in a form. Set a table: pick a deck, seat opponents, choose a bracket, and one bracket judges every seat — a list that is not a hundred is refused by number, and the game cannot start until it is out.

  • Nothing here writes to your library. A table being set up is not a fact about your collection.
  • Log a game when you are done and it lands in the deck's History, beside the measured runs.

Start here

Ten minutes to a library that knows where everything is.

1 · Come in through one of the two doors

Draft a deck in the Build tab, or import what you own from Cards. Either works first; most people end up doing both.

2 · Finalize one deck

A hundred cards and a commander is enough. Finalizing is the moment reservations start, and the moment the app can tell you what is short.

3 · Press ?

Every page has help behind the question mark, written for the page you are on — including the diagram above. Take a Tour in the header walks seven journeys, each step pointing at something real on screen.

4 · Back it up, on purpose

User Functions → Save a backup file, or e-mail the export to yourself from a phone. Your data lives in your browser — which means it is yours, and means nobody else is keeping a copy.

Works offline once loaded — app, card records and deck data are all cached, so it opens at a convention with no signal. Esc puts down whatever you picked up.

CrankMagic is a static page with no framework and no dependencies. The states, stages and flow described here are the app's own — the diagram in The spine is the one it shows behind the question mark on the Cards page.
