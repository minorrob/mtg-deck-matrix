## The intended product

A private Commander workshop: know what you own down to printing and copy, build a deck that expresses your intent, understand its strengths and limits, get the correct hundred into sleeves, improve it from play, and deliberately sell or trade copies. Collecting and playing should feel enjoyable; recordkeeping should feel dependable. The app should automate routine bookkeeping while preserving the choices a person would make manually.

Keep the static deployment, no framework, no build step, UMD modules and local persistence. The three existing HTML entry points can remain while sharing one navigation system, domain model and visual language. Public card data and optional advice packs remain static assets. User records never become repository content or telemetry.

The end-to-end experience is: set a deck brief and optionally import a collection → select a commander by name or mechanics/rank, or start from an imported hundred → test supported strategies against a chosen pod → accept or reject recommendations → finalize and reserve the hundred → acquire missing cards and pull owned copies → explicitly mark physical placement In deck → protect allocations from automatic reuse → log games and refine. Released owned copies automatically fulfill compatible outstanding needs or return to the bench. Receiving, ordering and placement can happen incrementally and in different sequences for each card. Sell / Trade and enriched export are parallel collection workflows, not separate inventory authorities.

- Shared, testable lineup and slot projections; deterministic simulation with a published protocol and unseen-seed comparisons.
- Card-name resolution with explicit questions, manual records and exact-match promotion.
- Measured-score provenance, stale-score handling, composite breakdowns and restrained game-log conclusions.
- Mobile Shop, export/import, first-visit and collection-scale browser journeys, and the event-based card graph.
- The new `guide-agent.js`, its validators and `tools/generate-guides.mjs`. Extend this work instead of rebuilding it under another model's name.
- The upstream `pilot-policy.js`, measured policy ablations and “How you play it” panel. Extend these rather than recreate them. Their scores compare declared policies inside the current approximate model; they are not evidence that a human is playing incorrectly. The [simulation plan](simulation-fidelity-plan.md) records the remaining resource-accounting and information-access problems.
- Existing CSV/XLSX intake, offline `xlsx-writer.js` and deck-workbook projections. Extend their metadata coverage and preserve printing fidelity; do not replace functioning file support with instructions to do the work in another tool.

## The weakest part: a plausible answer can look like a verified fact

**Ownership is the first priority.** `deck-store.js:193` creates a catalog row with `actual[deckId] = card.quantity`, `own = card.quantity`, status `In Hand`, and zero to buy. `build-panel.js:313` sends generated decks through that store too. A direct reproduction using a generated one-card record and an empty catalog returned Sol Ring as owned and in hand without any inventory entry. An explicit inventory upload can override these projections, but it must not be required to prevent inferred ownership in the first place.

The inventory parser also rounds fractional quantities, substitutes one for an invalid quantity, and merges rows by normalized name while retaining only the first row's printing metadata (`inventory-import.js:103`, `:129`). These are concrete conflicts with the requested level of collection fidelity, even though the current tests pass.

**The largest intelligence gap is model validity.** The engine models opponent profiles and scheduled threats, not three actual opposing decks, and lacks a full legal-action/priority model. Recent commits fixed the earlier printed power/toughness hydration defect and uniformly remeasured the 200 main rungs; the subsequent `118fe39` also changes the engine to support pilot policies. Retain those improvements. The core profile-based approximation remains, and the committed game-history file contains zero human games. More seeds reduce sampling noise within the model; they cannot establish its accuracy against four real people. The detailed simulation plan identifies ten mechanisms that can change recommendations, qualifies the partially corrected held-answer behavior, and adds the pilot information/resource findings and their verification needs.

Two further risks shape the plan: My Decks and Matrix have different deck/ownership sources; and the full iterative optimizer still relies on local Node tools/result imports, whereas browser measurement runs synchronously. The proposed seamless experience requires changes beneath the interface.

## Delivery sequence and acceptance gates

### 1. Make ownership trustworthy and build the redesigned shell

First implementation PR: stop deriving ownership from a decklist. Default an import or generation to a **planned deck**. An explicit “these are physical cards I own” path previews the inventory change before committing it. Existing inferred holdings are preserved as legacy, unverified claims during migration, with a reconciliation report; never silently discarded or promoted to verified records.

Introduce a shared collection domain with separate card identities, printings, holdings/lots, pending orders, reservations, physical locations, deck plans and sleeved snapshots. Use Oracle identity for deck legality and printing identity for collection details. Permit “printing unknown”; never invent a set, finish or condition to fill a required field. Support aggregate lots for bulk basics and individual copy records where useful.

Every mutation has an operation ID, origin and timestamp. The user-facing Source is Owned, Ordered or To buy. Purpose is Main deck, an Upgrade linked to a replacement slot, or a Bracket bump linked to a slot/bundle and base/target bracket. Allocation and physical placement are independent. Finalization reserves 100 slot commitments, linking existing owned copies or orders and creating To buy requirements for shortfalls. It never creates an order or an owned card.

Transfers move allocations; reservations do not create ownership. An ordered copy is not in hand. A card planned for two decks can produce two requirements but cannot satisfy both from one physical copy. On a swap, a released owned copy automatically fulfills a compatible, committed To buy requirement in another deck; otherwise it returns to the bench. Match acceptable printings and quantities, respect user destination choices, and disclose priority when several decks need the same copy. Existing orders remain orders. The actual physical location stays recorded until moved, even when the new allocation is Bench or another deck. Donor-deck shortfalls and purchase-queue reductions are explicit, and one Undo reverses all related effects.

Separate visibility from optimizer eligibility. Every owned copy is inspectable, including copies In deck, reserved elsewhere, locked or designated Sell / Trade. Bench-only simulation excludes other decks' In deck/reserved copies, unreceived acquisitions and purchases unless the user enables those pools. Sell / Trade bench copies remain eligible by default. “Use cards In deck elsewhere” permits proposals, never automatic physical moves. A locked donor remains protected from automatic reuse unless individually opted in. A manual transfer is allowed after a hard warning naming the exact copy, donor shortfall and protection being overridden; the selected exception does not unlock the whole deck. Revalidate allocation revisions before accepting a stale simulation result.

Sell / Trade is explicit membership attached to owned copies, not a duplicated holding. Available offers remain bench candidates. A pending deal creates a separate commitment that automatic deck selection must respect; cancelling/revising it is an explicit choice. Completed dispositions reduce only the confirmed printing/quantity and preserve history. Agreed inbound trades remain non-owned, labeled Incoming trade until received. Declaring an offer, archiving a deck and accepting a recommendation do not create or destroy holdings.

Imports become staged transactions: preserve source rows; map columns; validate quantities; resolve identity; show duplicates, conflicts and skipped rows; preview the change; commit once. Re-importing the same batch is idempotent. Separate “add acquisitions” from “reconcile a full collection snapshot” so absence from a partial file never means disposal. Undo reverses an operation visibly.

**Gate:** generate/import with an empty collection → zero owned; three copies across five decks → three allocations and two shortages; the same file twice → unchanged totals; printing variants remain distinct; released copies fulfill only compatible unmet commitments; bench-only results never consume excluded donors; every owned copy stays inspectable; intentional transfer warnings name donor effects; sale/trade quantities change only on confirmed transactions; order/receive/undo and transfer/undo conserve the correct quantities; all pages agree.

### 2. Finish the complete visual facelift and durable local storage

The visual direction is a Commander workshop with Satoshi for the interface and an angular Oxanium sans-serif wordmark, graphite surfaces, clear blue accents and small amber highlights. The app is always dark: no light mode, system-theme inheritance or theme selector, including on first paint and in native form controls. No green or purple foundation palette and no serif typography. The requested green Forest-symbol completion orbs are a deliberate status accent; blue remains the app foundation. A shelf of commander artwork, crisp display type and tactile card compositions give the app its character. Dense collection rows remain restrained and legible. Natural colors within card artwork are retained; acquisition and placement always use text labels. Art must have a graceful offline fallback.

The selected CrankMagic brand mark is the [transparent wand variant](assets/crankmagic-logo-wand-v3.png): a blue C-shaped gear with five integrated mana glyphs, a long wand crossing behind blue/black/red cards, and a five-pronged crest on the front card. Its embedded 256px derivative has real transparency, preserves the black card and slightly brightens the narrow outer contour. Use no background tile. Keep the wordmark as real text in Oxanium 700, with an icy azure gradient on the fixed dark surface. Satoshi remains the face for the exact current subline and all other interface text. Both fonts must work offline. The [brand asset record](assets/README.md) includes asset provenance and the font license.

Unify the logo, wordmark and aether through their shared blue light. The accepted mist direction uses a fuzzy, diffuse helix with widely spaced turns around the subline, rather than a stroked line. Translucent front and rear layers convey the wrapping motion while preserving text readability. A growing proportion of fine fibers peel away across the final stretch beneath the text; by the former transition point after the final word, all fibers have transitioned. Fade the remaining mist in proportion to that separation so there is no abrupt junction. Keep the flow centered vertically on the subtext line and allow equal upward and downward drift: no lower floor or upward bias. The effect occupies existing header space and must add no header height. Adapt to the final text line when the subline wraps. Provide a keyboard-accessible pause/resume control beside the wordmark, render a still wisp under reduced-motion preferences, and suspend animation while the header is offscreen or the document is hidden. Keep this decorative; it conveys no loading, validation or simulation state.

Replace the header’s top-right study text and theme selector with **User Functions**, available from every main view. Carry over the existing main-branch Admin capabilities: Export a backup, Import a backup, Load default, Undo the last load, Reset picks and Clear session (renamed **Clear all data**). Include the planned enriched Excel export. Show save/last-backup status inside the menu, with the undo action enabled only when a valid recovery snapshot exists. Preserve the difference between resetting picks and deleting a collection. Imports/default loads preview their scope and retain a recovery snapshot; supplied decklists never imply owned cards. Clear all data offers a complete backup and then a separate explicit confirmation naming the affected data; remove only CrankMagic-owned storage, and ensure reload remains empty. Keep menus usable by keyboard, Escape, outside click and touch. The design study previews these operations; it does not claim to perform full backup, restore, Excel export or deletion.

### 3. Establish a defensible measurement baseline

Preserve the now-fixed printed-body hydration and uniform main-rung measurements. Freeze reproducible manifests for the current baseline; reconcile stale fidelity documentation and quarantine incomparable older alternative results. Audit canonical facts across acquisition/storage/hydration paths, especially faces, variable `*` values, colors and abilities. A back-face body is not automatically the active front face. Fetch any missing public generation inputs only when rebuilding that pipeline, with provenance.

Introduce a capability report: supported, approximated or unsupported mechanics and which win conditions matter to this deck. Unsupported strategies can retain valid mana/curve measurements but cannot receive a confident overall win recommendation. Use one versioned card-feature representation, preserving the tests that prevent classifier drift.

A measurement identity includes deck version/hash, card-fact hash, engine version, classifier version, rules/bracket snapshot, opponent snapshot, policy, objective and seed protocol. Changing any of these invalidates comparable results. Display both sampling uncertainty and model coverage; they answer different questions.

The recent fixed-list remeasurement is the baseline, not work to repeat without cause. Further data/rules corrections require paired fixed-list comparisons before re-optimization, then coherent regeneration of dependent ratings, rung evidence and guides. Retain the legacy six-seed × 20,000-game and 2,000-game preview protocols for reproducing legacy results. Benchmark the new four-player kernel before setting its sample budgets; adaptive candidate screening and untouched finalist confirmation need separately versioned protocols. Respect runner stop conditions and compute budgets; no ledger resets or limit edits to force a result.

Move browser computation into cancellable Web Workers using the same UMD engine. Add progress, bounded batches, local result caching and a revision guard so a late result cannot attach to a changed deck.

**Gate:** fixed-fixture regressions, browser/Node parity, deterministic replay, restored-data coverage, coherent score provenance, UI responsive during a full run, and cancellation that cannot save a partial run as final.

### 4. Add real opposing decks and extend the existing pilot policies

Make public Archidekt deck URLs, user-provided lists and shareable opponent packs the inputs to an opponent library. Preserve source URL, retrieved date, exact list and content hash. Cache locally and use export/paste if live fetch or browser CORS fails. External lists are data, never instructions. Do not scrape private decks or upload the user's inventory.

An initial deck-derived profile can improve scouting but must be labeled as a profile. It is not completion of the requested deck-versus-deck simulator. The actual target is four explicit deck states with libraries, hands, mana, battlefield, graveyard, life, commander state and per-seat decision policies.

Build the legal state/action kernel first, including stack/priority, mana payment, zones, source lifetimes, triggers, replacement effects, state-based actions and outcome accounting for all four seats. Add actual bodies/blocks/combat and expand supported mechanics through hand-auditable scenarios, then validated spell chains and combo patterns. Never carry deck-wide bonuses from an undrawn or removed source. Validate each increment. A broad, perfectly accurate Commander rules engine is not a credible single-release promise; unsupported cards remain visible and block overconfident optimization.

Pilot policy changes decisions, not merely score weights: mulligans, sequencing, threat assessment, attack targets, holding interaction and committing resources. Keep tactical policy separate from deck archetype and bracket. Rotate seats and opponent mixes; use the same policy machinery for all seats. Record selected game traces so a surprising result can be inspected. Enforce private-observation boundaries: pilots cannot see opponents’ hidden cards or future random draws. Model politics as bounded, disclosed sensitivity scenarios, not simulated human conversation.

Start with a small diverse benchmark of public lists, including combat, tokens, aristocrats, voltron, control and spell/combo strategies, with explicit exclusions where coverage is incomplete. Add equal-deck symmetry, seat-rotation and counterexample tests. Release broad matchup claims only after the supported strategy families pass their benchmarks.

**Gate:** a report really identifies the three opposing lists and policies that were played; equivalent seats behave symmetrically over repeated trials; inspected replays follow the supported rules; unsupported win conditions are never quietly scored as weak decks.

### 5. Build the intent-driven improvement loop

Persist a typed brief: commander/theme, pinned cards, prohibited strategies, base bracket, bracket ceiling, total purchase cap, optional per-card cap, reuse constraints, play style, competitiveness, speed, saltiness, complexity and pilot approach. Deck locks protect donor allocations and are distinct from pinned cards in the optimized list. Sliders remain approachable controls, but budget maps to visible dollars and measurable targets show their units. Saltiness becomes explicit restrictions and named proxies; it must not be equated with competitiveness. Never silently raise a ceiling to accommodate a result.

Separate hard constraints from soft preferences. “Owned only,” the selected eligible-copy pool, a maximum budget, pinned cards and legality cannot be silently sacrificed to return a hundred. If no complete feasible deck exists, show an explicitly incomplete plan, the conflicting requirements and proposed relaxations for the user to choose. Protected donor allocations require the user's explicit opt-in before the solver may include them; inclusion in a result still does not authorize an unreviewed transfer. Speed and complexity can be optimized as preferences; complexity remains an operational proxy until validated.

The loop is: baseline → diagnose failures → propose legal candidate swaps → preview → retain promising alternatives → full confirmation on unseen seeds and opponent lists → report. Return a small Pareto set, such as best fit, minimum spend and most reuse, rather than hiding the tradeoffs inside one score. Constrain expected performance and weak-matchup downside without claiming a solved game-theoretic equilibrium.

Use paired runs and controlled card substitutions. “Win rate when cast” is useful descriptive evidence, but a card cast in winning games is not necessarily the cause of those wins. Do not automatically cut a strategy piece because the current simulator cannot use it. Keep holdout opponents and seeds out of candidate selection; a final holdout result is not another tuning signal.

SWOT findings become structured records: evidence, matchup, supported mechanism, candidate response, cost/availability, measured difference and uncertainty. Accepting recommendations creates a new plan revision; sleeving is a separate physical action. Stop when gains are not practically meaningful, confidence is insufficient or the budget is exhausted. Report the best tested feasible list and why the run stopped.

**Gate:** zero hard-constraint violations; all named cards resolve and are legal under the pinned rule snapshot; gains survive unseen tests; unsupported decks can yield “insufficient evidence”; every accepted swap is reversible and leaves ownership unchanged.

### 6. Add low-cost AI explanations and earned calibration

The existing engine is Monte Carlo simulation plus heuristic search, not trained ML. First make that system sound. A later local model can rank candidate swaps or calibrate predictions from opted-in local game logs. It must beat a simple baseline on held-out data before affecting recommendations. Training only on the simulator would mainly learn its assumptions; real-game validation is needed to claim better real-world prediction. Start with simple regularized models and versioned coefficients, not browser-scale reinforcement learning.

Game logs should capture the exact deck version, opponents when known, pilot, seat, outcome, turns and a short failure reason. Distinguish evidence about a particular pod from universal evidence. A simulated policy gap provides coaching hypotheses, not proof that a person's piloting caused a loss.

Use AI after deterministic work: explain a measured tradeoff, produce a concise play guide, translate a brief into proposed structured preferences, or suggest candidate interpretations of an unresolved name. Card lookup still ends in authoritative identity matching and user confirmation; an AI guess never creates a verified card or ownership record. Scryfall cache/registry/lookup remains the normal path.

The best fit for the strict static constraint is a local API tool that exports validated advice packs, plus generated guides committed as public content where appropriate. Your key stays in the local environment. The public app downloads ordinary JSON; no runtime token cost. For private decks, import the generated pack locally instead of publishing it. Ordinary browser code cannot safely hide an owner-funded API key ([OpenAI authentication guidance](https://developers.openai.com/api/reference/overview#authentication)).

Live AI inside the app requires provider-supported browser access and an explicit user opt-in to a personal credential flow. Session-only key handling is the proposed default if that option is enabled; the key remains accessible to page scripts and cannot be treated as a hidden shared secret. If the provider does not support direct browser access, use imported advice packs or user-run local tooling. An application backend is outside this plan. The application remains fully functional with AI off.

Cost controls are architectural: no model call per simulated game, optimization iteration, keystroke or page load; deterministic findings before prose; bounded context drawn from the relevant cards; structured output; one bounded repair attempt; cache by deck/evidence/prompt/model version; record usage. Proposed pilot spending ceiling: $2 total, with a default $0.05 maximum reserved cost per guide including a retry. These are proposed limits, not claimed prices or approval to spend. Benchmark the user's available provider/model on real tricky cases, then choose the least expensive option meeting the quality bar. If no option fits, use templates or show the gap rather than exceed the cap. Both existing-card references and proposed upgrade references must pass deterministic checks; current prose heuristics alone cannot prove tactical correctness.

**Gate:** measured arithmetic never comes from the model; invalid names/references are rejected; missing facts stay unknown; hostile deck names cannot influence tools or expose a key; repeated identical requests cost zero additional tokens; AI failure leaves local workflows working; no private request is sent without the user's opt-in.

## What sign-off authorizes and how the work progresses

Sign-off on this package will authorize implementation against the specified final behavior. It does not require another approval for each reversible engineering step. The hard boundary remains a static app with local-only user persistence. Optional paid API use, publication, and merging are separate from completing a reviewable branch.

Use six ordered milestones with measurable exits:

1. **Truth and shell:** inventory/commitment separation, migration/reconciliation, shared CrankMagic components, copy-conservation regressions and the first complete acquire-to-In deck journey.
2. **Collection and navigation:** complete overview/Collection flow, groups and imports, Shop, swaps/reallocation, archive, Sell / Trade, backup/restore and enriched Excel, with end-to-end failure/undo tests.
3. **Rules and four seats:** baseline manifests, support gates, worker infrastructure, four complete supported lists, legal actions and inspectable replays. This is the first reliable point to estimate remaining simulator coverage and compute cost.
4. **Coverage and intelligence:** the user's deck families, real opponent packs, varied pilots, graph exploration, Copilot findings, constrained candidate generation and paired holdout comparisons. Unsupported families stay explicitly unavailable for overall outcome recommendations.
5. **Calibration and optional learning:** practical local logs and human-position review, prospective held-out evaluation, calibrated estimates only where earned, optional lightweight learned policies. Instrumentation and uncalibrated supported simulation can ship before sufficient human data exists; human-accuracy claims cannot.
6. **Acceptance and branch completion:** run the full release matrix, repair failures, reconcile docs/assets/generated manifests, review the final diff, and prepare the draft PR with evidence and known limits. No merge.

These are dependency gates, not invented calendar promises. Four-player Commander rules coverage and acquiring adequate human validation data are the largest uncertainties. If a human dataset is unavailable, the honest deliverable is a rules-tested, explicitly uncalibrated lab plus logging/evaluation tooling; calibration remains unfinished and no human-realism percentage is advertised. Quantify narrower supported cohorts rather than lowering the gate to label the whole simulator complete.

The proposed product can return **no feasible deck**, **unsupported interaction**, **insufficient evidence**, or **keep the current list**. These are successful truthful outcomes when constraints or evidence require them. It must not produce an attractive but invented answer simply to fill the screen.

Use an integration feature branch with reviewable milestone commits or subordinate branches, never work on or merge to `main`.
